Vector bundles on Fargues-Fontaine curves

Kiran S. Kedlaya

1 Abstract complete curves and vector bundles

Definition 1
#

By an abstract complete curve we mean a connected, separated, regular \(1\)-dimensional Noetherian scheme \(X\), together with a degree function from closed points of \(X\) to positive integers with the property that the induced linear map \(\deg \colon \operatorname{Div}(X) \to \mathbb {Z}\) is zero on all principal divisors.

Definition 2
#

Uses

  • Definition 1

Since the degree map \(\operatorname{Div}(X) \to \mathbb {Z}\) vanishes on principal divisors, it induces a map \(\operatorname{Pic}(X) \to \mathbb {Z}\) which we use to define a degree homomorphism \(\deg \colon K_0(X) \to \mathbb {Z}\).

Definition 3
#

Uses

  • Definition 1

Let \(F \subseteq G\) be an inclusion of vector bundles on \(X\). Then there is a unique intermediate bundle \(F'\) such that \(\operatorname{rank}(F) = \operatorname{rank}(F')\) and \(G/F'\) is a vector bundle, called the saturation of \(F\) in \(G\). We then have \(\deg (F) \leq \deg (F')\) with equality if and only if \(F' = F\).

Lemma 4
#

Uses

  • Definition 1

Every vector bundle \(F\) on \(X\) admits a filtration of the form

\[ 0 = F_0 \subset \cdots \subset F_n = F \]

in which \(F_i/F_{i-1}\) is a vector bundle of rank \(1\) for \(i=1,\dots ,n\).

Proof ▶

We proceed by induction on \(\operatorname{rank}(F)\), the case of rank 0 serving as a trivial base case. For the induction step, use the ampleness of \(\mathcal{O}_X(1)\) to construct a surjection \(\mathcal{O}_X^{\oplus d} \to F(m)\) for some positive integers \(d\) and \(m\). In particular, there exists a nonzero (hence injective) map \(\mathcal{O}_X(m) \to F\) for some integer \(m\); let \(F_1\) be the saturation of the image of this map. We then apply the induction hypothesis to \(F/F_1\) to conclude.

Definition 5
#

Uses

  • Definition 1

Let \(F\) be a nonzero vector bundle on \(X\). We define the slope of \(F\) as

\[ \mu (F) := \frac{\deg (F)}{\operatorname{rank}(F)}. \]

The bundle \(F\) is stable if there is no nonzero proper subbundle \(G\) of \(F\) with \(\mu (G) \geq \mu (F)\). The bundle \(F\) is semistable if there is no nonzero subbundle \(G\) of \(F\) with \(\mu (G) {\gt} \mu (F)\). The bundle \(F\) is polystable if it is a direct sum of stable vector bundles, all of the same slope.

Lemma 6
#

Uses

  • Definition 1

Let \(F,G\) be two stable vector bundles on \(X\) of the same slope \(\mu \). Then any nonzero morphism \(f\colon F \to G\) is an isomorphism.

Proof ▶

Let \(H \neq 0\) be the image of \(f\). Let \(H'\) be the saturation of \(H\) in \(G\). Then

\[ \mu = \mu (F) \leq \mu (H) \leq \mu (H') \leq \mu (G) = \mu \]

so all of these inequalities must be equalities. Since \(G\) is stable this means that \(H = H' = G\), and since \(F\) is stable the kernel of \(f\) must be zero.

Proposition 7
#

Uses

  • Definition 1

Every vector bundle \(F\) on \(X\) admits a unique filtration

\[ 0 = F_0 \subset F_1 \subset \cdots \subset F_l = F \]

in which each successive quotient \(F_i/F_{i-1}\) is a semistable vector bundle of some slope \(\mu _i\) and \(\mu _1 {\gt} \cdots {\gt} \mu _l\).

Proof ▶
Definition 8
#

Uses

  • Definition 1

For \(F\) a vector bundle on \(X\), the filtration on \(F\) described in ??prop:HN filtration is called the Harder–Narasimhan filtration, or HN filtration, of \(F\). We define the multiset of HN slopes of \(F\) to consist of \(\mu _i = \deg (F_i/F_{i-1})/\operatorname{rank}(F_i/F_{i-1})\) with multiplicity \(\operatorname{rank}(F_i/F_{i-1})\).

Lemma 9
#

Uses

  • Definition 1

Let \(F,G\) be two nonzero vector bundles on \(X\). If \(F \otimes G\) is semistable, then so are \(F\) and \(G\).

Proof ▶

Let \(F_1, G_1\) be the first steps in the respective HN filtrations of \(F\) and \(G\); then \(\mu (F_1) \geq \mu (F)\), \(\mu (G_1) \geq \mu (G)\). Then \(F_1 \otimes G_1\) is a nonzero subbundle of \(F \otimes G\) of slope \(\mu (F_1)+\mu (G_1)\); since \(F \otimes G\) is semistable, we must have

\[ \mu (F_1)+\mu (G_1) \leq \mu (F \otimes G) = \mu (F) + \mu (G) \leq \mu (F_1) + \mu (G_1). \]

We conclude that \(\mu (F_1) = \mu (F), \mu (G_1) = \mu (G)\) and so \(F\) and \(G\) are semistable.

2 Classification of vector bundles

Definition 10
#

Let \(P := \bigoplus _{d \geq 0} P_d\) be a graded ring satisfying the following conditions.

  • The subring \(P_0\) of \(P\) is a field and \(P_1 \neq 0\).

  • The multiplicative monoid

    \[ \left( \bigcup _{n=0}^\infty P_n \setminus \{ 0\} \right)/P_0^\times \]

    is generated by \((P_1 \setminus \{ 0\} )/P_{0}^{\times }\).

  • For each nonzero \(s \in P_1\), there exists a graded ring isomorphism

    \begin{equation} \label{eq:graded ring isom mod s} P/sP \cong P_0 \oplus T L_s[T] \end{equation}
    1

    for some field \(L_s\) containing \(P_0\).

Proposition 11
#

The degree map \(\deg \colon \operatorname{Div}(X) \to \mathbb {Z}\) induces an isomorphism \(\operatorname{Pic}(X) \cong \mathbb {Z}\).

Proof ▶

The map \(\operatorname{Pic}(X) \to \mathbb {Z}\) carries \(\mathcal{O}_X(n)\) to \(n\), so we need only check injectivity. This follows from the fact that for any nonzero \(s \in P_1\), \(P[s^{-1}]_0\) is a principal ideal domain.

Lemma 12
#

We have \(H^i(X, \mathcal{O}_X(n)) = 0\) for all \(n \geq 0\).

Proof ▶

For any \(s,t \in P_1\) which are linearly independent over \(P_0\), by 1 we have \(P_2 = P_1 s + P_1 t\). By induction on \(m+n+k\) we deduce that \(P_{m+n+k} = P_{m+k} s^n + P_{n+k} t^m\) for any \(m,n {\gt}0\) and \(k \geq 0\), and hence

\[ H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(n)) = \frac{P[(st)^{-1}]_n}{P[s^{-1}]_n + P[t^{-1}]_n} = \varinjlim _m \frac{P_{n+2m}}{P_{n+m}s^m + P_{n+m} t^m} = 0. \qedhere \]
Lemma 13
#

Every vector bundle \(F\) on \(X\) admits a filtration of the form

\[ 0 = F_0 \subset \cdots \subset F_n = F \]

in which each successive quotient \(F_i/F_{i-1}\) is isomorphic to \(\mathcal{O}_X(m_i)\) for some \(m_i \in \mathbb {Z}\).

Proof ▶

Consider the filtration in Lemma 13. By ??prop:abstract curve2, each successive quotient is isomorphic to \(\mathcal{O}_X(m_i)\) for some \(m_i \in \mathbb {Z}\).

Lemma 14
#

Let \(F\) be a vector bundle on \(X\) admitting a filtration with successive quotients \(\mathcal{O}_X(-1), \mathcal{O}_X^{\oplus n}, \mathcal{O}_X(1)\) for some \(n \geq 0\). Then \(H^0(X, F) \neq 0\).

Proof ▶

Choose \(s \in P_1\) nonzero and let \(x \in |X|\) be the unique closed point at which \(s\) vanishes. Define the bundle \(H\) to fit into the commutative diagram

\[ \begin{tikzcd} \end{tikzcd} 0 \arrow {r} & \mathcal{O}_X(-1) \arrow {r} \arrow {d} & F \arrow {r}\arrow {d} & F/\mathcal{O}_X(-1) \arrow {r} \arrow [d,equal] & 0 \\ 0 \arrow {r} & \mathcal{O}_X \arrow {r} & H \arrow {r} & F/\mathcal{O}_X(-1) \arrow {r} & 0 \end{tikzcd} \]

in which the left vertical arrow is injective with cokernel supported at \(x\). Since \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_X(\mathcal{O}_X, \mathcal{O}_X) = H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X)\) vanishes by Lemma 12, \(H\) admits a filtration with successive quotients \(\mathcal{O}_X^{\oplus (n+1)}, \mathcal{O}_X(1)\).

We may assume that \(H^0(X, H(-1)) = 0\), as otherwise \(H\) contains the subbundle \(\mathcal{O}_X(1)\) whose intersection with \(F\) has degree \(\geq 0\) and hence by Proposition 11 is either \(\mathcal{O}_X\) or \(\mathcal{O}_X(1)\). Then \(H^0(X, H)\) injects into \(H^0(X, H/H(-1))\), which we reinterpret as the fiber \(H_x\) of \(H\) at \(x\). Using the exact sequence

\[ 0 \to H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X^{\oplus n+1}) \to H^0(X, H) \to H^0(X, \mathcal{O}_X(1)) \to H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X^{\oplus n}) = 0 \]

(where the last equality comes from ??lem:H1 of line bundles), we express the image of \(H^0(X, H) \to H_x\) as \(P_0 e_1 + \cdots + P_0 e_{n+1} + L_s e_{n+2}\) for some basis \(e_1,\dots ,e_{n+2}\) of \(H_x\) over \(L_s\).

Meanwhile, the image of \(H^0(X, F) \subset H^0(X, H)\) in \(H_x\) is the intersection of the image of \(H^0(X, H)\) with the image of \(F_x \to H_x\); the latter is a codimension 1 subspace of \(H_x\) and so can be written as the kernel of a nonzero \(L_s\)-linear functional \(\lambda \colon H_x \to L_s\). Define \(a \in P_0\), \(b \in L_s\) by

\[ (a,b) := \begin{cases} (0, 1) & \lambda (e_{n+2}) = 0 \\ (1, -\lambda (e_1)/\lambda (e_{n+2})) & \lambda (e_{n+2}) \neq 0; \end{cases} \]

then \(ae_1 + be_{n+2}\) is the image in \(H_x\) of a nonzero element of \(H^0(X, F)\).

Lemma 15
#

With notation as in Lemma 13, we can further ensure that

\[ m_j \leq m_i+1 \qquad (1 \leq i {\lt} j \leq n). \]
Proof ▶

Among filtrations as in Lemma 13, choose one to minimize \(\sum _{i=1}^n (n-i) m_i\). We prove that this filtration has the desired property by induction on \(j-i\).

We start with the base case \(j=i+1\). Suppose by way of contradiction that this fails for some \(i\). We then have an exact sequence

\[ 0 \to \mathcal{O}_X(m_i) \to F_{i+1}/F_{i-1} \to \mathcal{O}_X(m_{i+1}) \to 0. \]

By choosing an embedding \(\mathcal{O}_X(m_i+2) \to \mathcal{O}_X(m_{i+1})\), we obtain a subbundle \(G\) of \(F_{i+1}/F_{i-1}\) and an exact sequence

\[ 0 \to \mathcal{O}_X(m_i) \to G \to \mathcal{O}_X(m_i+2) \to 0. \]

By Lemma 14, \(G\) admits \(\mathcal{O}_X(m_{i}+1)\) as a (not necessarily saturated) subbundle. We can thus replace the terms \(m_i, m_{i+1}\) in our original sequence by new terms \(m_i', m_{i+1}'\) with \(m_i' {\gt} m_i\) and \(m_i' + m_{i+1}' = m_i + m_{i+1}\), contradicting minimality.

We next proceed to the induction step \(j-i{\gt} 1\). Suppose by way of contradiction that this fails for some \(i\). By the induction hypothesis, we have \(m_{j-1} \leq m_i+1\) and \(m_j \leq m_{j-1}+1\), so both of these must be equalities; in particular, \(m_j = m_i + 2\). For \(k=i+1,\dots ,j-1\), the induction hypothesis yields \(m_i + 1 \leq m_k \leq m_j-1\), so the sequence \((m_i, \dots , m_j)\) looks like \((m-1, m, \dots , m, m+1)\) for some \(m\). That is, \(F_j/F_i\) admits a filtration with successive quotients \(\mathcal{O}_X(m-1), \mathcal{O}_X(m)^{\oplus (j-i-1)}, \mathcal{O}_X(m+1)\). By Lemma 14 again, \(F_j/F_i\) admits \(\mathcal{O}_X(m)\) as a (not necessarily saturated) subbundle, but again this would contradict minimality.

Proposition 16
#

Every vector bundle on \(X\) which is semistable of slope \(0\) is trivial.

Proof ▶

Let \(F\) be a semistable vector bundle of degree \(0\) and rank \(n\), and choose a filtration as in Lemma 15. Since \(\sum _{i=1}^n m_i = \deg (F) = 0\), we have

\[ 0 \leq \max _i\{ m_i\} \leq m_1 + 1 \]

and so \(m_1 \geq -1\); moreover, if \(m_1 = -1\) we deduce that \(\sum _{i=1}^n m_i {\lt} m_1 + \sum _{i=2}^n m_i \leq -1 + 0 {\lt} 0\), a contradiction. Hence \(m_1 = 0\), and similar logic yields \(m_n = 0\); by semistability we deduce that \(m_1 = \cdots = m_n = 0\). Since \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_X(\mathcal{O}_X, \mathcal{O}_X) = H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X)\) vanishes by Lemma 12, it follows that \(F\) is trivial.

Lemma 17
#

Let \(F\) be a vector bundle on \(X\) with all HN slopes in \([0,1)\). Then for any closed point \(x \in |X|\), for \(j_x \colon x \to X\) the canonical inclusion, there exist a coherent sheaf \(G\) on \(x\) and an exact sequence

\[ 0 \to \mathcal{O}_X^{\oplus \operatorname{rank}(F)} \to F \to j_x^* G \to 0 \]

of coherent sheaves on \(X\).

Proof ▶

We show that for \(i=1,\dots ,\deg (F)\), we can find an exact sequence

\begin{equation} \label{eq:modification} 0 \to H_i \to F \to j_x^* G_i \to 0 \end{equation}
4

in which \(H_i\) is a vector bundle of the same rank \(F\) with degree \(\deg (F)-i\) and all HN slopes nonnegative (and hence in \([0,1)\)). For \(i = \deg (F)\), \(H_i\) will then be semistable of degree \(0\), and hence trivial by Proposition 16.

In the case \(i=1\), it suffices to check the claim after replacing \(F\) with its maximum quotient having all HN slopes in \((0,1)\); at this point, every exact sequence of the form 4, in which \(H_1\) is a vector bundle of the same rank \(F\) with degree \(\deg (F)-1\), has the property that the HN slopes of \(H_1\) are nonnegative. We achieve the general case by applying this argument repeatedly.

Corollary 18
#

Let \(F\) be a nonzero vector bundle on \(X\) whose HN slopes are all nonnegative. Then \(F\) is generated by global sections and \(H^1(X, F) = 0\).

Proof ▶

By replacing \(F\) with the first step of its HN filtration, we may reduce to the case where \(F\) is semistable. By twisting, we may further ensure that \(\mu (F) \in [0,1)\). We then deduce both claims from Lemma 12 and Lemma 17.

Lemma 19
#

For any semistable vector bundle \(F\) on \(X\), \(F^\vee \otimes F\) is trivial.

Proof ▶

We immediately reduce to the case where \(F\) is stable with slope in \([0,1)\). In this case, suppose by way of contradiction that \(F^\vee \otimes F\) is not semistable, and let \(G\) be the first step of its HN filtration. Since \(G \otimes G\) has slope \(2\mu (G) {\gt} \mu (G)\), the composition map \((F^\vee \otimes F) \otimes (F^\vee \otimes F) \to F^\vee \otimes F\) restricts to zero on \(G \otimes G\). By Corollary 18, \(G\) is generated by global sections; consequently, \(G \otimes G\) is generated by nonzero global sections of the form \(s_1 \otimes s_2\). Any such section corresponds to a pair of nonzero endomorphisms \(F \to F\) which compose to zero; this contradicts Lemma 6. We deduce that \(F^\vee \otimes F\) is semistable of slope \(0\), and hence trivial by Proposition 16.

Proposition 20
#

The tensor product of any two semistable vector bundles on \(X\) is semistable.

Proof ▶

Let \(F,G\) be two semistable vector bundles on \(X\). By Lemma 19,

\[ (F^\vee \otimes G^\vee ) \otimes (F \otimes G) \cong (F^\vee \otimes F) \otimes (G^\vee \otimes G) \]

is semistable. By Lemma 9, \(F \otimes G\) must be semistable.

Lemma 21
#

Any exact sequence of vector bundles on \(X\) of the form

\[ 0 \to F_1 \to F \to F_2 \to 0, \]

in which every HN slope of \(F_1\) is greater than or equal to every HN slope of \(F_2\), is split.

Proof ▶

By Proposition 20, \(F_2^\vee \otimes F_1\) has nonnegative HN slopes. Now \(\operatorname{Ext}^1_X(F_2, F_1) = H^1(X, F_2^\vee \otimes F_1)\) which vanishes by Corollary 18.

Proposition 22
#

Every vector bundle on \(X\) splits (nonuniquely) as a direct sum of stable bundles. In particular, the HN filtration always splits and every semistable bundle is polystable (i.e., a direct sum of stable bundles of the same slope).

Proof ▶

Both assertions follow directly from Lemma 21.

Lemma 23
#

For \(d\) a positive integer with \(\dim _{P_0} P_1 {\gt} d\) and \(c \in \mathbb {Z}\), there exists a semistable vector bundle on \(X\) of rank \(d\) and degree \(c\).

Proof ▶

We may assume without loss of generality that \(d{\gt}1\) and \(c \in \{ 1,\dots ,d-1\} \). Choose \(s \in P_1\) nonzero and let \(x \in |X|\) be the unique closed point at which \(s\) vanishes. Set \(F := \mathcal{O}_X^{\oplus d}\) and let \(e_1,\dots ,e_d\) be the images in \(F_x\) of the standard basis vectors of \(H^0(X, F) \cong P_0^{d}\). The condition \(\dim _{P_0} P_1 {\gt} d\) is equivalent to \(\dim _{P_0} L_s \geq d\); consequently, we can choose elements \(f_1,\dots ,f_c \in L_s\) such that \(1,f_1,\dots ,f_c\) are linearly independent over \(P_0\). Let \(V \subset F_x\) be the \(L_s\)-span of \(e_1 - f_1 e_d, \dots , e_c - f_d e_d\) and construct the subbundle \(G\) of \(F\) so that we have an exact sequence

\[ 0 \to G \to F \to j_x^* V \to 0; \]

then \(G^\vee \) is semistable of degree \(c\).

Lemma 24
#

For any \(\lambda \in \mathbb {Q}\), there is at most one isomorphism class of stable vector bundles on \(X\) of slope \(\lambda \).

Proof ▶

Let \(F,G\) be two such bundles. By Proposition 20, \(F^\vee \otimes G\) is trivial. We can thus find a nonzero morphism \(f\colon F \to G\), which must be an isomorphism by Lemma 6.

Theorem 25
#

Set

\[ S := \begin{cases} \mathbb {Z}& \mbox{in the classical case,} \\ \tfrac {1}{2} \mathbb {Z}& \mbox{in the twistor case,} \\ \mathbb {Q}& \mbox{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

Then the following statements hold.

  1. There exists exactly one isomorphism class of stable vector bundles \(\mathcal{O}_X(\lambda )\) of slope \(\lambda \) if \(\lambda \in S\) and none if \(\lambda \notin S\).

  2. For \(\lambda \in S\) with denominator \(d\) in lower terms, \(\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{O}_X(\lambda )) = d\).

  3. The endomorphisms of \(\mathcal{O}_X(\lambda )\) form a division algebra of degree \(d\) over \(P_0\).

  4. For \(\lambda , \lambda ' \in S\), \(\mathcal{O}_X(\lambda ) \otimes \mathcal{O}_X(\lambda ') \cong \mathcal{O}_X(\lambda +\lambda ')^{\oplus m}\) for some positive integer \(m\).

  5. Every vector bundle on \(X\) splits (nonuniquely) as a direct sum of summands each of the form \(\mathcal{O}_X(\lambda )\) for some \(\lambda \in S\).

Proof ▶

If \(\dim _{P_0} P_1 = \infty \), then we may deduce (a) and (b) from Lemma 23 and Lemma 24. Otherwise, as per Remark ?? we may argue as follows.

  • In the classical case, \(P = P_0[x,y]\) and \(H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X(-1)) = 0\). Hence any exact sequence of the form

    \[ 0 \to \mathcal{O}_X \to F \to \mathcal{O}_X(1) \to 0 \]

    splits, as then does any filtration as in Lemma 15, proving (a). From this, (b) is obvious.

  • In the twistor case, by identifying \(P \otimes _{P_0} P_0(\sqrt{-1})\) with \(P_0(\sqrt{-1})[x^2,xy,y^2]\) and applying the classical case, we see that \(S \subseteq \tfrac {1}{2}\mathbb {Z}\). We deduce (a) and (b) by applying Lemma 23 to construct a stable bundle of rank \(2\) and degree \(1\), then applying Lemma 24 for uniqueness.

Given (a)–(b), (c) follows from Lemma 6 and Lemma 19; (d) is immediate from Proposition 20 and Proposition 22; and (e) is immediate from Proposition 22.
